# Global X Camera — complete pre-layout design review

> **Scope as of October 13, 2026:** this is the September 5 review of the recovered VD55G0/TPS62132/AO3401A circuit, which remains the implemented schematic. The selected prototype now uses CAM-6G3-073CLR/VD56G3 and LTC3621; see the [current specification](../spec.md) and [power implementation study](../power.md). Findings remain open until corrected or explicitly superseded with evidence. The sensor-specific timing, energy and capacity calculations below do not qualify the new module; historical discussion recommendations are not the current decision queue.

**Reviewed 2026-09-05. The current design needs electrical and footprint corrections before it should be routed or fabricated.** The architecture remains worth prototyping, but battery pulse capability and the cold-start capture budget need early measurements. Improving placement alone will not resolve the faults below.

This review covers architecture, power paths, all component references, connected MCU alternate functions and voltage domains, camera/USB/storage interfaces, clocks/reset/debug, startup/shutdown, firmware-dependent behavior, part selection, critical footprint geometry, and mechanical/routing feasibility. It uses the native Flux snapshot and reconstructed physical netlist, with manufacturer datasheets, reference manuals, errata and reference-driver evidence. It does not assume that the older specification's verification claims or a zero-error ERC establish a working circuit. **The schematic and footprints have not been changed by this review.**

The full evidence is split into [power](review/power-review.md), [MCU/pin-by-pin](review/mcu-review.md), [camera and memory](review/camera-memory-review.md), [USB/RTC/accelerometer/system](review/system-review.md), and [critical footprint measurements](review/footprint-spot-check.md). ## First corrections

| Priority | Finding and effect | Recommended correction | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The shutter latch drives a battery-level signal into PC13.** D3 connects the gate pull-down network to the MCU input. Keeping the input within its allowed voltage can also keep the PFETs conducting, defeating shutdown. | Separate low-voltage shutter sensing from the battery-level latch actuator. Redesign this together with supply sequencing and USB selection. | PWR-001, [power review](review/power-review.md) |
| 2 | **The boot flash chip-select is on the wrong pin for ROM boot.** PN12/P14 is NCS2; the ROM expects PN1/R17, NCS1. | Move BOOT_XSPI_NCS and its pull-up to PN1/R17. Firmware in the unaddressed flash cannot fix this initial boot step. | MCU-001, [ROM pin evidence](review/mcu-review.md) |
| 3 | **Main and auxiliary MCU supplies are tied together where independent sequencing is required.** The common 1V8 net includes AON and internal-SMPS/PMU/other auxiliary inputs. | Split main/AON and auxiliary supplies; use PWR_ON to control the auxiliary group and provide bounded discharge before main power is removed. Include USB insertion/removal. | MCU-002, [supply review](review/mcu-review.md) |
| 4 | **The inherited J3 camera connector lands have incorrect geometry.** Signal pads are0.127×1.1176 mm; the Hirose recommended geometry is approximately 0.18×0.31 mm. They extend into the central no-conductor region. | Rebuild the complete land/mask/paste pattern from the exact drawing and independently verify mating orientation. | CAM-001, [drawing comparison](review/camera-memory-review.md) |
| 5 | **NAND ready/busy is connected to FMC_NWAIT, not FMC_RNB.** The intended NAND hardware wait/sequencer path is unavailable there. | Move from PF2/N4 to PA7/R12, AF12 FMC_RNB on the 3.3 V bank. Software polling is a possible separately validated workaround, not the intended connection. | MCU-003, [pin review](review/mcu-review.md) |
| 6 | **RTC interrupt is pulled to 3.3 V into a 1.8 V bank.** PH2 belongs to VDDIO4; this exceeds its recommended input operating limit and can exceed the absolute limit in1.8 V mode. | Pull the open-drain interrupt to 1.8 V or move it to a 3.3 V bank. | MCU-004, [voltage limits](review/mcu-review.md) |
| 7 | **NRST capacitance exceeds the device limit.** C38 is 100 nF; the datasheet maximum is 10 nF. | Choose a value whose tolerance remains at or below 10 nF; verify reset pulses and applicable silicon errata. | MCU-005, [reset evidence](review/mcu-review.md) |
| 8 | **PFET gate drive has no absolute-maximum margin.** At13.2 V, the divider nominally produces12 V across a ±12 V rated gate. | Revise the divider, select a more suitable PFET, or add a characterized clamp while preserving off-state leakage. | PWR-002, [gate calculation](review/power-review.md) |
| 9 | **USB startup automatically reconnects the batteries.** Dedicated PWR_ON asserts before firmware and turns on the battery PFETs; the higher-voltage cells then supply the session. | Add deliberate USB/battery priority and reverse-current control. A diode from VBUS does not provide that priority. | PWR-003, [source-path analysis](review/power-review.md) |
| 10 | **The core inductor selection is inadequate and local bypass allocation is incomplete.** L3 has a 2 A target and generic0603 footprint; current ST guidance requires Isat>3 A and DCR<60 mΩ. | Select actual magnetics/MLCCs and allocate the complete local SMPS input bank before placing the MCU power section. | PWR-005/006, [power passives](review/power-review.md) |

“Confirmed” means an observed connection, geometry or selection conflicts with a documented requirement, or an explicit release input is missing. “Conditional” means the result depends on an unprovided configuration, part revision or operating choice. “Measurement” means qualification is needed; it is not a claim that every implementation fails. A blocker prevents the relevant boot, architecture or manufacturing milestone; it does not mean every subsystem is unusable.

## Architecture decisions to resolve early

**Power delivery is the largest feasibility question.** The provisional1.2 A load at 3.3 V is 3.96 W. At an illustrative90% converter efficiency, a four-cell series pack must provide333–550 mA through **each cell** over 13.2–8 V. The reference CR2032 document does not qualify that workload. Actual capture power may be lower, so a measured representative waveform is essential. The 210 µF nominal3.3 V reservoir supplies only about 0.198 mJ between3.3 and 3.0 V—roughly50 µs at 3.96 W. It cannot bridge a 50 ms capture at that power. Select the cells and contacts, characterize cold/aged/weak-cell behavior, and measure capture plus save energy before optimizing layout. The 10-year shelf target also requires a complete leakage/aging budget, not typical regulator currents. See PWR-004/007/008.

**The 50 ms target needs an explicit cold-start budget.** ROM reads this NOR through a single-bit interface with an ideal4 MB/s ceiling:100 KiB takes at least25.6 ms and 200 KiB at least51.2 ms before overhead. The official camera driver loads6,868 patch bytes for sensor cut1 versus 260 for cut2. At1 MHz I2C the payload-plus-ACK lower bounds are61.812 ms and 2.340 ms respectively. Cut1's documented patch path alone exceeds the deadline. Qualify the sensor revision, use a small capture-first FSBL, overlap accelerometer startup, and defer storage scans/erase/download tasks. Define whether50 ms ends at exposure completion or at receipt of a complete valid frame; bound exposure and cold autoexposure convergence. See MCU-006, CAM-002/003/007, ACC-001 and MEM-003.

**Keep the N657 for the initial feasibility prototype.** Native CSI and SRAM are useful here, and replacing it now would introduce another unverified design. Its multiple supplies, cold boot and BGA are real costs, so run only the clocks/blocks the workload needs. The 0.81 V rail label is the low-voltage operating point, supporting up to 600 MHz;800 MHz requires the higher core-voltage setting. Measure whether the higher frequency helps energy-to-capture rather than assuming maximum clock is necessary. See MCU-007 and SYS-002.

**The selected NAND is compatible with N6 BCH capability.** It needs4-bit ECC per 512-byte data sector plus the associated spare-area requirement; STM32N6 supports BCH4/8. This is not a one-bit-ECC incompatibility. Protect metadata that lies outside the FMC BCH data path, preserve bad-block markers, define append-only commits and recovery after interrupted program/erase, and retain erased space in advance. Full native RAW10 is 486,220 packed bytes per image; the NAND-only program busy estimate is approximately 71–143 ms per image, outside the acquisition deadline but must be included in the measured energy budget. The AC part remains listed for new designs in Macronix's March2026 longevity list; an AD substitute changes ECC/spare geometry and requires separate qualification. See MEM-001–005.

## Coverage and practical limits

The reviewed baseline has 148 components,737 physical terminals,550 connected endpoints,187 explicit no-connects and 81 named nets. All 41 connected GPIO/oscillator pads were checked against physical ball, alternate function and supply bank. The prior264-ball map check remains part of the evidence. All 147 host footprints load and cover703 host pads, while the actual dimension spot checks are separately enumerated. CAM1 is an external module; optional SW2/SW3 contain documented inferred connections.

Several connections are consistent: camera lane/polarity and 1.8 V control functions, NOR legacy-SPI compatibility, NAND data-bus voltage, USB D+/D−/CC topology, RTC backup topology, accelerometer I2C mode/address, the main buck feedback arrangements and LED polarity. These successful checks are documented alongside the failures. No completed KiCad PCB, measured rail waveforms, SI/EMI/ESD data or integrated firmware were available, so this is not a board-performance or manufacturing signoff. Firmware integration does not block the known circuit/footprint corrections.

## Closure plan

1. **Correct and review the electrical design:** latch/source selection, main/aux supply sequencing, boot CS, NAND RNB, RTC interrupt voltage, reset capacitor, magnetics/local bypass and camera power-down control. Re-run ERC and an intentional-change-aware netlist comparison.
2. **Prove feasibility on representative hardware:** selected batteries/contacts, a minimal boot/capture workload, identified camera cut, real exposure policy and measured shutter-to-frame timing. Log rail minima, complete acquisition/save energy and off-state current over the chosen temperature/end-of-life envelope.
3. **Close mechanics and manufacturing inputs:** corrected J3, complete power-passive MPNs, camera model/physical fit, final thickness, representative fabricator stackup and BGA escape trial.
4. **Implement placement/routing and validate:** local decoupling and regulator loops first; CSI/USB/FMC/XSPI constraints; DRC, ground-return inspection, power-integrity and timing checks, assembly drawings and independent footprint review.
5. **Bring up and stress the first board:** battery/USB source combinations, rapid shutter retries, startup/shutdown/brownout, warm-reset NOR state, random NAND power interruption, RTC retention, both USB orientations and speeds, image quality/orientation, thermal and ESD testing. Record explicit pass criteria and measured traces rather than only “works on bench.”

For discussion, start with the latch and power sequencing. They are coupled and influence the battery experiment and every other subsystem. Isolated shutter sensing plus deliberate source/rail control adds parts and requires leakage accounting, but gives defined voltage domains and reliable shutdown. The complete report remains available so later issues can be addressed one at a time.
