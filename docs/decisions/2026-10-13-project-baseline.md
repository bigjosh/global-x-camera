See the [ongoing tests and current image state](../../firmware/boot-timing/optimization-results-2026-10-17.md).

This is the durable decision register for Global X Camera. It consolidates the October 12–18 review and points to the repository's engineering evidence. The repository, rather than a chat or Page, is the project record. Current requirements are implemented in documentation, not yet in a revised circuit or integrated firmware.

**October 17 measured update — CN8 power cycling:** **CN8 → CN9 pin 3 → JS110 → CN9 pin 2** supports API-controlled target cycling: three recorded repeats booted autonomously, with **15.20–15.43 ms from NRST rising to PD0**, or **22.67–22.90 ms from the target rail crossing 0.5 V**. Both measured rails were below 0.1 V together for about 13 seconds beforehand. A one-time `HWRSTPULSE` released an initially stuck-low NRST; no debugger command occurred during the repeat cycles. Its original cause remains unresolved. The roughly 4 ms interval from the late 4.50 V crossing excludes the earlier ramp and is not full cold-boot time. The board stayed powered after releasing the JS110 API connection. See the [October 17 bench record](../../firmware/boot-timing/bench-results-2026-10-17.md). No sensor/exposure compliance is established; the October 16 results below remain evidence for the earlier ST-LINK-powered setup.

**October 16 discovery — autonomous boot timing:** a 256-byte first-GPIO payload, packaged as a 1,280-byte boot image, now runs from the Nucleo's external NOR. Two observed power-ups reached PD0 **62.75–62.91 ms after the first 4.50 V target-rail crossing**. Roughly 50 ms elapsed before the observed NRST rising edge; **12.92–13.43 ms** followed it. Identify the source of the active reset hold before treating this as an MCU limitation. JS110 reconnection also caused the upstream source to collapse; manual USB reconnection recovered it. The original NOR was backed up before programming its first sector; no OTP/security settings changed. See the [full bench record](../../firmware/boot-timing/bench-results-2026-10-16.md). D-04 remains a requirement; no exposure-start compliance is established.

## Engineering discoveries and boundaries

### Sensor, optics and prototype hardware

- **October 15 physical bring-up:** the user received the hardware, mounted CAM-6G3-073CLR on NUCLEO-N657X0-Q and connected ST-Link USB. After the user moved only JP2 to pins 2–3 for development mode, a RAM-only test produced recognizable 640 × 480 Y8 snapshots at 10 ms and unity gain. Actual sensor model/revision/ROM: `0x5603` / `0x3110` / `0x0420`, selecting the inspected embedded driver's fast-boot path. Code, exact setup and repeat results are in [firmware/bringup](../../firmware/bringup/README.md). One CSI D-PHY control error occurred earlier in each observed startup; final snapshots had no new errors. This proves a basic capture path, not full-resolution RAW10, 50 ms compliance, battery operation, storage or PTP. No OTP/flash programming or power/debug rework was performed. The board-arrival prerequisite is met; supply modifications still require their own verified setup.
- **VD56G3 frame payload:** 1124 × 1364 × 10 / 8 = **1,916,420 bytes** before metadata, alignment and spare capacity. Two hundred payloads need **383,284,000 bytes (about 365.5 MiB)**. A nominal 4 Gbit / 512 MiB device holds 280 bare payloads; an illustrative 2 MiB slot allocation with 20% reserve leaves 204 slots. That narrow planning margin is not qualified usable capacity. Bad blocks, reserved metadata, erase geometry and recovery must be included.
- The former VD55G0 workload is 644 × 604 RAW10 = **486,220 bytes**. The earlier approximately 800-image capacity and NAND timing/energy tables do not qualify the larger selected camera. See the [NAND comparison](../power/quad-nand-comparison.md) and [system-energy model](../power/nand-energy-impact.md).
- Complete camera-module stock in quantity 500 was **not confirmed** for any reviewed candidate. Dated distributor counts are snapshots, not allocation or supplier commitments. Module optical height does not include all PCB/enclosure structure. The selected 4.68 mm module makes a complete ≤5 mm product mechanically demanding.
- VD5943's tall supplied module does not define the bare sensor's minimum optical-package height. Kantatsu F6639/D0501 and other compact-lens leads, crop calculations, chief-ray-angle concerns, and the distinction between optical TTL and complete module thickness are preserved in the [prototype research record](../prototype/prototype-parts-and-camera-research.md). No compatibility or supply claim is inferred from an old lens catalog.
- **NUCLEO-N657X0-Q** is the selected base for the user's self-assembled first prototype (D-35). It has no microSD socket; onboard NOR is not a substitute for qualification of the eventual photo-storage device. Its target USB port is **CN8**; **CN10** is ST-LINK. Verify board jumper/power configuration before external-rail injection. [ST UM3417](https://www.st.com/resource/en/user_manual/um3417-stm32n6-nucleo144-board-mb1940-stmicroelectronics.pdf).
- The user confirmed placing a DigiKey order (message 014). Its exact purchased quantities and substitutions were not supplied. The shopping recommendations, including camera adapter, RTC/accelerometer boards, LTC3621 demo board, NAND board and candidate chips, are preserved locally; they are not an inferred purchase receipt or frozen production BOM.

### Power-tree fundamentals — October 14 review, decision updated October 18

The [power-tree review](../power/2026-10-14-power-tree-review.md) records the rail requirements and sources. **D-47 now selects 5.0 V for the Nucleo prototype with existing downstream regulation.** The Nucleo generates MCU main/AON/AUX/core rails; the camera adapter generates **2.8 V / 1.8 V / 1.15 V** from 3.3 V. Camera power is only functionally needed through frame readout; independent gating requires verification. A custom-board 3.3 V front end remains a candidate. The [assembly guide](../prototype/nucleo-assembly-guide.md) supplies the current rework path; DC1863A identity is confirmed, while sequencing, HOLD margin, peak loads, isolation and leakage still need implementation/qualification. No applied CAD or hardware change is implied.

**Direct 3.3 V alternative, not selected:** the [feasibility study](../power/2026-10-14-power-tree-review.md#direct-33-v-nucleo-operation--feasibility-study) retains main/AUX regulator-bypass ideas for later comparison. D-47 selects 5.0 V for the present prototype; do not apply those bypasses. No factory 3.3 V input-jumper mode or safe one-cut modification was established.

**Power-cost follow-up:** the user requested a comparison of standard 5 V Nucleo, modified 3.3 V Nucleo and a custom low-power board. The [sourced calculation](../power/power-cost-comparison.md) estimates 0.39, 0.22 and 0.15 J/photo respectively for a common assumed 0.86 s cycle; standard-board debugger current is an explicit unmeasured 30 mA example. A 5 V reference with debugger isolated is 0.25 J. Removing the debugger and power LED in both board cases leaves about a 7% conversion saving at 3.3 V. The recommendation is initial 5 V bring-up, simple overhead removal and measurement before supply rework. Typical NAND timing suggests about 0.70 s to write/read back a RAW frame at 100 MHz quad before software/control-record overhead. These are engineering estimates, not a voltage choice, measured energy or demonstrated latency.

### RUN control, leakage and shutdown

- **Debugger measurement/isolation follow-up, October 14:** the user asked to establish how to isolate the Nucleo debug section. The [investigation](../power/nucleo-debug-isolation.md) confirms CN12 rail shunts but no documented complete debugger-off jumper or breakaway procedure. D2 is the candidate main-supply cut; target-facing debug, serial, reset, clock and voltage-sense paths also require review for standalone qualification. JP3 is reset and CN9 selects sources. Exact rework and verification on the delivered board remain implementation work; debugger-free energy estimates are not yet demonstrated.

- LTC3621 shutdown is **0.1 µA typical, 1 µA maximum** at the cited 12 V/25°C test condition. Its typical value alone uses the entire project main-path target before external leakage; screen and measure the assembled stage rather than assume compliance. The enabled converter's quiescent current also counts while the button is held. [Local ADI datasheet](../../datasheets/candidates/power-latch/ADI_LTC3621.pdf).
- The study's 330 kΩ/100 kΩ button divider draws about **18.6–30.7 µA over the assumed 8–13.2 V pack range**. A diode-fed 3.3 V HOLD GPIO has different margin from a 1.8 V GPIO. Neither the pack endpoint nor the final resistor/diode choice has been qualified. See [power.md](../../power.md).
- **Standby makes ordinary STM32N6 GPIOs high impedance without pull-ups/pull-downs; Stop retains I/O configuration.** Select a non-exception HOLD pin and retain external pulldowns. `PWR_ON` and internal SMPS behavior do not remove the obligation to discharge external AUX rails while required main/AON rails remain valid. A dedicated external sequencer is not inherently mandatory. [Local RM0486](../../datasheets/processor/STM32N6_RM0486_reference_manual.pdf), §13.7.7, and [AN5967](../../datasheets/processor/STM32N6_AN5967_hardware_design.pdf).
- RTC backup bypasses the main regulator through one cell. In the retained RV-3028-C8 Level Switching Mode, the cited manufacturer table gives **140 nA typical / 210 nA maximum** at 3 V/25°C with VDD below 2 V. This additional cell load differs from lower VDD-only timekeeping figures. Preserve primary-cell charging disable and unpowered-bus isolation. [Local RTC application manual](../../datasheets/sensors_timing/RV-3028-C8_application_manual.pdf), p97.
- Firmware ignoring a USB-mode press cannot stop the physical divider from raising RUN. USB source detection, reverse-current isolation and hardware RUN suppression need a combined circuit; the HOLD diode alone does not provide them. Avoid battery-level or USB 5 V exposure to GPIOs, and avoid shorting a driven HOLD path through a RUN clamp.
- For FD-03, a bare series capacitor without reset can fail to rearm. A resistor across it gives reset after release but residual held current: **10 MΩ is an illustrative value yielding about 1.25 µA at 13 V**, not a selection. Larger resistance reduces current and slows reset. SPDT reset can avoid that intentional held-current path but needs transient protection. A negative pulse is topology-dependent; the floating-plate SPST arrangement does not inherently force one. RUN's absolute negative limit is −0.3 V. See the local ADI datasheet and messages 046–047.

### Storage integrity and firmware

- Native SLC NAND needs explicit bad-block, ECC, metadata, commit and interrupted-write recovery design. Disabling full-image readback does not remove those obligations. A program-status check is not a readback ECC result. Loss of power before a valid commit must not expose an incomplete photo as successfully saved; exact recovery implementation remains open.
- Micron's modeled write-only advantage over Winbond nearly disappears when full readback is included. MCU-on time and complete-save energy matter alongside NAND-only figures. Exact device/interface selection remains open; parallel NAND, 1.8 V quad NAND, 3.3 V quad NAND and microSD studies are candidates rather than adopted new pin maps.
- **Ten-year room-temperature retention without power/refresh (D-23), 1,000 complete reuse cycles (D-24), PTP/per-photo deletion (D-25), ECC/CRC-32C with damaged-photo recovery (D-26), and permanent block retirement (D-27) are accepted.** Internal raw NAND records need no host-visible filesystem. The `_RECOVERED` filename suffix and EXIF UserComment are approved in D-28. D-29 settles fresh, full NAND-to-RAM reads on every host request, without relocation or background image scans; predictive media errors retire the whole slot. D-31 stores photo pixels and attributes once; storage-control redundancy remains implementation work. See the [read policy](ecc-read-error-review.md).
- Rust was explored, not selected. No recovered integrated camera firmware was found. The firmware framework should be chosen against boot, capture, storage, USB and power behavior, not assumed from a prior language discussion.

### Recovered design and manufacturing

- Flux recovery accounts for **148 components, 737 terminals and 81 nets** in the checked reconstructed design. Clean ERC and recovered footprint coverage establish model consistency, not electrical correctness or a production release. [Recovery](../../hardware/reconstruction/README.md), [current CAD evidence](../../research_notes/Global%20X%20Camera%20decision%20review/current-cad-evidence.json).
- The September review contains **47 findings: 5 blockers, 22 high, 17 medium, 3 low**. Preserve concrete corrections such as latch/shutter-sense voltage, boot flash chip select, parallel NAND ready/busy, RTC interrupt level, reset capacitance, camera connector geometry and external rail sequencing until fixed or superseded with evidence. [Finding register](../review/findings.md).
- Current KiCad files still contain VD55G0, 3.3 V memories, parallel NAND and TPS62132/TPS62825/AO3401A power circuitry. The accepted new camera/converter choices are not applied CAD changes. No finished routed PCB, manufacturing release or measured complete capture/save/shutdown cycle was found.
- JLCPCB Standard is a study recommendation, with PCBWay fallback; neither is selected. D-35 explicitly defers this choice while the user builds the first prototype around the Nucleo. Conventional four-layer construction is a working target, with six layers a proposed option if routing requires it. Stackup, BGA escape, panel/rails, assembler scope, quantity and final mechanical fit need qualification. [Manufacturing study](../manufacturing/china-pcba-feasibility.md).
