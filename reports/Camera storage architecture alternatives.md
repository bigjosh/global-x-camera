# Camera storage architecture alternatives

Research checked October 18, 2026. **Yes: two independent raw NAND chips, industrial microSD, and industrial pSLC eMMC are credible alternatives.** Two quad-SPI NAND chips are the strongest extension of our existing design: they double capacity and can overlap programming across chips, without either chip supporting cache programming. Industrial microSD is the easiest large-capacity architecture to try on the bench; eMMC offers a soldered production alternative with flexible interface voltage. Their advertised write speeds do not establish cold-save time or energy, because initialization, internal maintenance and durable shutdown also matter. **My recommendation is to investigate two-chip raw NAND for direct control and industrial microSD as the quickest comparison experiment.** The Alliance D-46 selection remains unchanged; this review makes no hardware change or purchase.

## Two chips can pipeline one photo without internal cache support

Use two **MX35LF4GE4AD-Z4I** devices on one quad bus with separate chip selects. Load a page into A and start programming; then load B while A is busy. Alternate pages of the **same image** between them. Merely alternating whole photographs would add capacity without shortening one save. Macronix's documented program operation continues after chip select rises, permitting this proposed independent-device schedule. ([Macronix datasheet](https://www.macronix.com/Lists/Datasheet/Attachments/9047/MX35LF4GE4AD,%203V,%204Gb,%20v1.6.pdf))

At an assumed achieved 50 MHz quad rate, loading 4 KiB takes 163.84 µs; programming takes 400 µs typical. Our **1,916,420-byte native RAW10 image** occupies 468 padded pages. One chip models at approximately **264 ms**; two staggered chips model at **132 ms**. Both exclude startup, commands, checksums, metadata, erase and readback. Two 4 Gbit chips provide **1 GiB gross**, or approximately 409 fixed 2 MiB slots after an illustrative 20% reserve; guaranteed capacity still needs bad-block and metadata accounting.

This retains internal ECC and native 3.3 V quad operation, but adds package area, bus loading and peak current. The allocator must map each image across both chips and declare FULL only after both sides complete successfully. The extra chip-select pin and physical wiring remain unverified. This is distinct from a stacked-die serial part whose die-selection rules forbid switching while busy. ([Micron example of busy-state die-selection restrictions](https://datasheet.lcsc.com/datasheet/pdf/dd5a5d073373a605b97087884de5e38b.pdf?productCode=C22029726))

## Managed storage buys gigabytes but changes the timing contract

All candidates below can exceed the minimum **200 native images**, about 383 MB of payload before overhead. The timing column deliberately distinguishes modeled transfer-plus-program time from **payload-only** figures: none is a measured complete cold-capture cycle.

| Architecture | Example capacity | Timing evidence for one native image | Energy uncertainty and integration |
|---|---|---|---|
| One faster quad-SPI SLC NAND | 512 MiB–1 GiB | **264 ms Macronix / 189 ms Micron**, typical transfer-plus-program models | Direct physical control; carrier and qualified quad wiring required |
| Two independent quad-SPI SLC NANDs | 1 GiB with two 4 Gbit devices | **132 ms** staggered transfer-plus-program model | Higher simultaneous current; added CS and cross-chip allocation |
| Parallel SLC NAND with true cache programming | 1 GiB example | **150–155 ms**, conditional pipeline model | More signals and host ECC; Nucleo pin/ECC implementation unqualified |
| Industrial true-SLC or permanent-pSLC microSD | 4–8 GB examples; larger families available | **76.7 ms payload-only lower bound** at 50 MHz four-bit SD | Unknown cold initialization, busy tails and flush cost; simple socket carrier |
| Industrial permanent-pSLC eMMC | 4–8 GB examples; larger families available | **48–55 ms benchmark-scaled payload time** at 35–40 MB/s | Startup/shutdown and FTL work remain; BGA carrier and SDMMC wiring |

The raw-NAND models and exact parts are documented in the [earlier comparison](Faster%20camera%20storage%20alternatives.md). Native SD at 50 MHz four-bit has a 25 MB/s raw ceiling; a card's 80 MB/s headline cannot be achieved in that mode. The eMMC estimate uses ISSI's cache-OFF **eight-bit SDR52** benchmark. Four-bit SDR52 itself tops out at 26 MB/s before overhead, so it cannot reproduce that eight-bit result. ([SD timing reference](https://www.mouser.com/datasheet/2/615/swissbit_02162021_S_56u_data_sheet-2013721.pdf), [ISSI pSLC eMMC datasheet](https://www.issi.com/WW/pdf/21-22EF04GP-08GP.pdf))

For a microSD experiment, **Kingston SDCIT2/8GB** uses pSLC; **ATP AF4GUDI-WACXM**, 4 GB, is a true-SLC comparison. Observed DigiKey stock/prices were **414 / $79.28** and **3,093 / $177.88**, respectively, not reservations. The **Adafruit 4682**, listed at $3.50, exposes the full native SD interface without level translators. Its socket needs 3 V-class signaling. A candidate SDMMC2 route exists on the Nucleo's CN3/VDDIO5 pins, but the final wiring, pull-ups and rail switching have not been issued or tested. ([Kingston specification](https://www.kingston.com/datasheets/sdcit2_en.pdf), [Kingston listing](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/kingston-technology/SDCIT2-8GB/28521680), [ATP listing](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/atp-electronics-inc/AF4GUDI-WACXM/3770355), [Adafruit carrier](https://www.adafruit.com/product/4682))

ISSI's **IS21/22EF04GP/08GP** offers 4/8 GB pSLC eMMC with **2.7–3.6 V main power and 1.8/3.3 V VCCQ**: genuine interface-voltage flexibility, unlike choosing separate 1.8 V or 3.3 V raw-NAND variants. The exact **IS21EF04GP-JQLI** listing showed zero stock; no production-matched carrier was established. STM32N657 supports SDMMC/eMMC with DMA and up to eight data bits, but not HS400. ([ISSI specifications](https://www.issi.com/WW/pdf/21-22EF04GP-08GP.pdf), [DigiKey](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/issi-integrated-silicon-solution-inc/IS21EF04GP-JQLI/24617480), [STM32 interface capabilities](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/stm32n657x0.pdf))

## Measure joules through durable shutdown, not standby current

Because we remove power between photographs, the meaningful metric is **battery energy from power-on to durable commit and shutdown**, plus peak-current compatibility with four CR2032 cells. Include initialization, transfer, programming/busy intervals, commit or flush, MCU/SRAM consumption and regulator losses. Faster storage can save whole-board energy even if its active current is higher.

For illustration, Macronix's 30 mA typical program current gives about **18.5 mJ** for 468 × 400 µs at 3.3 V, excluding everything else. Two chips perform approximately the same programming work in less elapsed time; their combined program-current maxima reach **80 mA**, before other loads or transient margin. Reducing board-on time by 132 ms saves another 13.2 mJ at a hypothetical 100 mW residual board load. These are models, not measurements. ([Macronix current and timing tables](https://www.macronix.com/Lists/Datasheet/Attachments/9047/MX35LF4GE4AD,%203V,%204Gb,%20v1.6.pdf))

Managed storage's hidden costs can dominate a short photo write. NXP cites generic eMMC/SD initialization around **100–200 ms typical**, with up to one second allowed; this is not a bound or measurement for our candidates. DMA completion is not proof of durability: wait for programming/status completion, perform eMMC flush and power-off notification where applicable, and follow each SD card's specified completion and cache rules. SD also permits substantial busy intervals, and optional caching changes shutdown obligations. ([NXP platform guidance](https://www.nxp.com.cn/docs/en/errata/IMX8X_1N95W.pdf), [SD Association cache behavior](https://www.sdcard.org/press/thoughtleadership/applications-in-action-introducing-the-newest-application-performance-class/))

Storage initialization can overlap independent camera work, but our current policy requires trustworthy free-space availability **before exposure**. If managed-media startup exceeds the 50 ms deadline, concurrency alone does not solve that dependency; an earlier persistent capacity record or explicit policy change would be needed. Benchmark cold startup, durable save, energy and current peaks near full capacity and after reuse. Precision energy comparison also awaits correction of the bench's known power-measurement bypass paths.

## Retention and physical recovery remain selection gates

**No reviewed candidate, including raw NAND, is yet qualified for ten years unpowered at room temperature after 1,000 complete reuse cycles.** Industrial SLC/pSLC is a useful starting point, not the guarantee. Swissbit explicitly distinguishes ten-year beginning-of-life retention from one year at end of life; powered refresh cannot satisfy our unpowered requirement. Obtain exact-part retention-versus-wear evidence rather than assuming managed storage can or cannot comply. ([Swissbit microSD comparison](https://www.swissbit.com/en/products/flash-storage/industrial-microsd-card))

SD/eMMC controllers hide physical ECC, remapping and garbage collection. We can retain logical objects, checksums and commit records without requiring FAT or changing PTP export, but physical-slot retirement and best-effort raw-page salvage would need a revised policy. Power-loss testing must prove previously committed photos survive interrupted writes and controller maintenance. ([Swissbit FTL explanation](https://www.swissbit.com/en/technology/storage-technology))

NOR is less attractive for saving: one concrete 2 Gbit part needs approximately **1.12 seconds of internal programming alone** for this image. Current serial MRAM examples reach 32 MiB and F-RAM 2 MiB, making them costly or numerous for bulk photographs. **Small F-RAM for allocation metadata plus NAND for images** remains useful, without accelerating payload programming or making multi-byte updates automatically atomic. USB flash adds host/controller overhead; UFS, SATA and NVMe require interfaces this MCU lacks. ([NOR timing](https://www.macronix.com/Lists/Datasheet/Attachments/8709/MX66UM2G45G,%201.8V,%202Gb,%20v1.0.pdf), [MRAM portfolio](https://www.everspin.com/products), [F-RAM portfolio](https://www.infineon.com/products/memories/f-ram-ferroelectric-ram/excelon-f-ram), [STM32 peripheral list](https://www.st.com/en/microcontrollers-microprocessors/stm32n657x0.html))

The next comparison should therefore be **raw NAND versus one industrial microSD implementation under the same cold-shot workload**. Two-chip raw NAND is the most direct speed/capacity extension; eMMC remains the soldered managed-storage candidate after measurements establish that its startup, durability and energy fit the camera.
