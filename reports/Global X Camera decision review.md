## Current position

**Startup optimization follow-up, October 17:** the CH5 probe now measures main MCU VDDIO. Reset releases around 2.2 V during the ramp; the first marker is about 23 ms after target 5 V crosses 0.5 V. Fixed JS110 measurement range did not meaningfully improve startup. The original test is already a 256-byte bare-metal payload. Experimental ROM/debug diagnostics and exact-sector recovery are recorded in the [ongoing optimization evidence](../firmware/boot-timing/optimization-results-2026-10-17.md); no minimum or exposure-timing compliance is claimed.

**CN8 power cycling, October 17:** **CN8 → CN9 pin 3 → JS110 → CN9 pin 2** supports API-controlled target cycling: three recorded repeats booted autonomously, with **15.20–15.43 ms from NRST rising to PD0**, or **22.67–22.90 ms from the target rail crossing 0.5 V**. Both measured rails stayed below 0.1 V together for about 13 seconds beforehand. A one-time `HWRSTPULSE` released an initially stuck-low NRST; no debugger command occurred during the repeats. The original reset-hold cause remains unresolved. The roughly 4 ms interval from the late 4.50 V crossing excludes earlier startup on the slow ramp. Three repeats are not a worst-case bound, and no sensor/exposure compliance is established. See the [October 17 bench record](../firmware/boot-timing/bench-results-2026-10-17.md).

**Historical autonomous boot timing, October 16 — ST-LINK source:** a minimal first-GPIO image booted from external NOR. Two starts reached PD0 **62.75–62.91 ms after the first 4.50 V target-rail crossing**, including an approximately 50 ms active NRST hold of unresolved origin. The observed NRST edge to PD0 took **12.92–13.43 ms**. This does not establish the 50 ms exposure target or an intrinsic MCU minimum. The [bench record](../firmware/boot-timing/bench-results-2026-10-16.md) includes waveforms, code, power-reference limits, original-flash backup and the failed automatic power-cycle experiment with that source. The CN8 follow-up above changes the power-test setup; it does not erase these earlier observations. The board has JP1/JP2 on pins 1–2 for standalone marker boot; the camera test remains available in development mode.

**First physical capture, October 15:** the delivered NUCLEO-N657X0-Q with mounted CAM-6G3-073CLR successfully captured recognizable monochrome images. The [reproducible RAM-only test](../firmware/bringup/README.md) uses ST-Link USB for power/debugging and retrieves 640 × 480 Y8 snapshots at 10 ms exposure and unity gain. Sensor model `0x5603`, revision `0x3110`, ROM `0x0420` confirm the inspected embedded driver's fast-boot revision on this unit. A startup CSI D-PHY control error remains to investigate; the final snapshots had no new errors. Full-resolution RAW10, battery operation, NAND, PTP and 50 ms cold-start compliance remain unverified. Only the development-mode jumper was moved; no OTP/external flash was programmed and no power/debug isolation rework was performed. The board is now in hand; the earlier arrival-dependent hold no longer blocks inspection, while circuit modifications remain separate work. Captured scene images remain local; source, procedure and numeric evidence are in the public repo.

**Power-startup review, October 15:** the [ranked rail risks](../docs/decisions/50ms-cold-start-review.md#power-and-rail-startup-follow-up--october-15) are battery sag/inrush causing slow or repeated startup, main/AON/AUX and reset sequencing, late camera power/clock start, and button-to-HOLD continuity. Recommend verifying one clean startup on the weakest allowed pack first. Camera oscillator startup is specified at 5 ms maximum and can potentially overlap MCU boot; parallel startup increases peak demand. Component typical timings do not establish 50 ms compliance. No new topology, capacitance or voltage decision is recorded; power/debug modifications remain deferred; the board has since arrived for USB-powered capture bring-up.

**D-44/D-45 — cold-start constraints, approved October 15:** use only the newest image-sensor revision, confirmed during factory commissioning. Optimize the bootstrap loader for minimum practical size and startup time, and optimize the size, placement and loading order of additional code. Start sensor initialization before loading code needed only later, such as NAND payload programming. Overlap later loading with sensor hardware waits where safe; do not require the full application to load before sensor initialization begins. Exact memory layout and staging remain implementation work, and the 50 ms target still needs measurement. Factory commissioning must record actual sensor identity/revision; production revision expectations and the supported fast-boot path must stay aligned. Remaining timing risks include loaded battery/rail startup and bounded allocation recovery. No measured compliance or physical board modification is implied.

**October 15 status:** the Nucleo has arrived and basic camera capture is demonstrated above. Prototype power/debug modifications and their verification remain separate work; no supply modification or additional voltage approval is implied.

**Debugger-isolation follow-up:** the user requested a realistic measurement arrangement. ST provides MCU-rail shunts, which exclude the debugger's separate supply branch; these do not measure the full camera. The [isolation study](../docs/power/nucleo-debug-isolation.md) identifies D2 as a candidate main debugger-supply disconnect and records the signal/sense paths that must also be verified for standalone battery operation. No complete debugger-off jumper or breakaway procedure is documented. Exact rework and measured isolation remain open.

**Power-cost estimate, October 14:** [the comparison and reproducible model](../docs/power/power-cost-comparison.md) estimate 0.39 J/photo for a standard 5 V Nucleo with an illustrative debugger load, 0.25 J with debugger isolated, 0.22 J for modified 3.3 V and 0.15 J for a custom PCB, using the same assumed cycle. Debugger current is unmeasured. Removing both debugger and power LED leaves about a 7% saving from the 3.3 V conversion. Recommendation: keep the existing 5 V tree for initial bring-up, investigate simple overhead removal and measure before more involved rework. This is an engineering proposal, not a voltage decision or measured budget.

The recovered product concept is a thin, credit-card-size monochrome global-shutter camera: a shutter press starts the electronics, one full-resolution image receives time and orientation metadata, storage completes, and main power turns off. USB provides downloads independently of the batteries. The documented priorities are acquisition latency, total shot energy, thickness, area and procurability, in that order. These are the coherent existing baseline; this audit does not treat every sentence in the specification as a separately approved user decision. ([Product and priorities](<../spec.md>))

The Flux recovery and KiCad migration are real accomplishments. The reconstruction accounts for 148 components, 737 terminals and 81 nets; it preserves the recovered connectivity and records its additions. A readable edition also exists. **On October 12, a fresh export of the current schematic matched the September reviewed connectivity baseline.** The current parts remain STM32N657X0H3Q, W25Q128JVPIQ boot NOR, MX30LF2G18AC-TI parallel NAND, TPS62132/TPS62825 regulators and AO3401A battery switches. The readable edition rearranges presentation; it does not implement the proposed fixes. The September register contains **47 findings: 5 blockers, 22 high, 17 medium and 3 low**. It distinguishes confirmed conflicts, conditional requirements and matters requiring measurement; these are not 47 independent product decisions. No completed routed PCB, manufacturing release, integrated camera firmware or measured complete capture/save/shutdown cycle was found. The zero-error ERC result demonstrates consistency of the reconstructed schematic model, not a working or manufacturable camera. No image relocation, refresh or background payload scans; only explicit host deletion removes photos. ### 1 Camera choice — settled for the prototype

**Settled October 12:** Use **CAM-6G3-073CLR (VD56G3), 73° diagonal field of view**, for the prototype. This replaces the earlier recommendation to retain VD55G0. Recalculate the dependent RAM, storage, capture timing, energy and mechanical budgets and implement the chosen module; no schematic change or measured performance is implied by the decision. ### 2 Shutter response target — settled for the prototype

**Settled:** From fully powered off, **exposure must start within 50 ms of the shutter button press**. The interval includes power-up, processor boot and camera initialization. Exposure duration, frame transfer, storage commit and shutdown may finish after this deadline and must be measured separately. This approval defines the requirement; it does not establish measured compliance. Verify the start of the exposure that produces the saved image, rather than a preliminary/discarded frame. Identify the module revision and instrument actual exposure start during cold-start tests, then record light/exposure conditions, timing variation and complete save/shutdown latency. ([Current specification](<../spec.md>))

### 3 Thickness and OFF-current budget — settled for the prototype

**Settled:** Four CR2032s in series through LTC3621 for the prototype; ≤5 mm is the finished-product target and a thicker functional prototype is allowed. The user has specified a **0.1 µA (100 nA) room-temperature OFF target for the main power path**, superseding the earlier <1 µA target. **RTC backup is retained and budgeted separately as additional cell drain**, by explicit user approval. **Proposed approach:** screen regulators and reject parts with excessive leakage; this is not yet a qualified screening process. **Implementation scope:** The 100 nA main-path allowance includes the regulator plus input-capacitor, switch/PCB and other pack-side leakage; it is not a regulator-only allowance. The recovered design powers RTC backup directly from one cell, and the power study identifies input-capacitor, switch/PCB and other battery-side leakage outside the regulator. Its RV-3028-C8 is configured for Level Switching Mode; the manufacturer's table gives 140 nA typical / 210 nA maximum backup current at 3 V and 25°C with VDD below 2 V. That is a separate cell load and cannot be removed by screening the buck. [RTC operating parameters, p97](https://www.microcrystal.com/fileadmin/Media/Products/RTC/App.Manual/RV-3028-C8_App-Manual.pdf#page=97). Test the assembled power stage at the intended pack voltage after settling, with appropriate fixture resolution, and record the RTC cell-tap current separately. Do not treat the regulator's typical shutdown number as a whole-camera result or assume screening yield. ([Battery and mechanics review](<../docs/DESIGN-REVIEW.md>), [OFF budget](<../power.md>))

### 4 Storage and data integrity

**Settled:** Retain at least **200 full-resolution, uncompressed packed-RAW10 photos between downloads**. Each VD56G3 image is 1,916,420 bytes before metadata. A 4 Gbit (512 MiB) device holds 280 tightly packed payloads before overhead; planning for 2 MiB per image and a 20% overall reserve gives 204 slots. These calculations do not qualify usable capacity or freeze an exact NAND part. The earlier approximately 800-image estimate applies to VD55G0 only. [Sensor format](https://www.st.com/resource/en/data_brief/cam-56g3.pdf). **Readback decision, October 13:** Full-image readback and checksum comparison are configurable, enabled during bring-up and initial testing, and may be disabled later. Both modes still require program-completion/status checks, integrity metadata/checksum generation, a verified final commit record and interrupted-write recovery. ECC status must be handled whenever data is read; no-read mode does not establish that the payload has been read back successfully. Benchmark complete save time and energy in both modes. The switch does not waive retention or power-interruption testing. **Storage-full decision, October 13:** Preserve existing photos and reject new captures when no usable image slot remains. D-42 selects seven red flashes then shutdown, without a new photo; reclaim space through an explicit USB erase action after download. Connecting USB or downloading alone does not erase photos. The usable-capacity minimum remains 200 photos; D-42 selects seven red flashes, while flash timing and safe PTP deletion remain implementation work. **Failure-policy decisions, October 13:** Mark allocation USED before payload saving and DONE on successful completion. Retain interrupted bytes as occupied/possibly corrupt; preserve prior photos and do not automatically erase/reuse suspect data. Report existing-photo read and explicit-erase errors over USB. Store BAD time/orientation values and omit the corresponding unavailable EXIF fields during USB file generation. The physical format and conservative recovery remain to implement. **Retention decision, October 14:** Retain successfully committed photos and recovery metadata for ten years at room temperature (approximately 25°C reference), fully unpowered, with no refresh needed. No periodic waking, battery power or USB maintenance may be required. Qualify this through the accepted 1,000 complete reuse cycles; no compliance claim is established yet. **Reuse decision, October 14:** Support 1,000 complete fill/download/explicit-erase cycles, equivalent to at least 200,000 photo saves at 200 photos per fill. Retention still applies to images saved after rated usage. Exact memory/interface and retention qualification remain to complete. **Recommendation:** Qualify the proposed 1.8 V quad-NAND path, retaining a qualified alternative, before declaring a winner. Use the final image format and a common complete-save test, including metadata, ECC/status checks, verification and shutdown. Implement the accepted power-interruption, retention, 1,000-cycle reuse and PTP/per-photo-deletion requirements. **Read policy approved and refined October 14:** each host image-data request freshly reads the whole original NAND image into RAM, checks ECC/CRC-32C, then exports that request's buffer with filename/EXIF warnings. Fully corrected/CRC-valid pixels remain verified; damaged bytes are exported best-effort. Predictive media errors permanently retire the whole slot from future writes without deleting its current image or preventing later host reads. Later reads may differ. No image relocation, refresh, background payload scans or substitution of an earlier request's RAM buffer; the USB copy-out proposal is superseded. Only explicit host deletion removes photos; retirement persists afterward. Catalog metadata reads and capture-time verification remain separate. Current-read PTP filename timing, RAM capacity and predictive thresholds need qualification. The object policy is settled: store pixels and photo attributes together once, without separate copies. Storage-control redundancy is implementation-dependent. D-32 approves best-effort retrieval and explicit deletion of PARTIAL objects with EXIF and/or PTP warnings. D-33 provisionally accepts circular first-FREE-and-GOOD allocation after the last durable allocation, with the cursor recovered through the journal/checkpoints. The page/block storage layout and off-NAND table location explicitly remain open for a later implementation review. The tradeoff is important: Micron’s modeled write-only advantage nearly disappears against Winbond when full readback is included, and longer MCU-on time can outweigh lower NAND energy. microSD adds a switched 3.3 V rail and socket, with cold-start and durable-commit behavior needing qualification. Close with exact-part documentation/supply and representative fault-injection and energy results. ([Quad NAND comparison](<../docs/power/quad-nand-comparison.md>), [whole-system energy](<../docs/power/nand-energy-impact.md>), [reliability concerns](https://chatgpt.com/c/6aa5d95a-b2e8-83ea-933a-5f18d637cad3), [microSD assessment](<../docs/power/microsd-feasibility.md>))

### 5 Power and shutter control

**Settled:** LTC3621 supplied by four series CR2032 cells is the prototype front-end regulator direction. **Accepted control direction, October 13:** Use the button-fed battery divider to assert RUN, with a diode from GPIO to RUN providing HOLD after early firmware takeover. The user accepts a minimum press duration until HOLD is established and accepts low-power waiting after the save if the button remains held; main power then switches off on release. This follows the existing RUN/HOLD study. Forced power-off while the button remains held is not required for the prototype. **Future direction FD-03, October 13:** retain a series-capacitor start pulse as a possible defense against held-button battery drain; reset/rearming, reliable boot-to-HOLD timing and leakage/transients need qualification. This is separate from the optional RUN-to-ground shutdown-delay capacitor below. The held state includes divider current, the enabled regulator's quiescent consumption, converted MCU/peripheral loads and separate RTC backup; it is not the 100 nA button-released OFF state. **User clarification:** after a successful safe save, firmware prepares peripherals and the shutdown state, then enters the lowest-power mode; the intended HOLD release occurs as part of that transition. RM0486 Rev 4 §13.7.7 states that ordinary GPIOs become high impedance without pull-up/down in Standby, subject to its listed pin exceptions; Stop retains I/O configuration. Select a suitable ordinary HOLD pin and retain the external pulldown so Standby itself can release the diode-fed HOLD. An optional capacitor from RUN to ground may delay buck disable after HOLD releases, but no value is selected. Coordinate the resulting delay with auxiliary-rail discharge before main supplies fall, and check startup latency, component tolerances/leakage and rapid button release/repress. The capacitor holds RUN enabled; the battery and regulator continue supplying the MCU during that interval. Exact timing, held-button Standby current and the integrated shutdown circuit remain unqualified. [RM0486 §13.7.7, p383](https://www.st.com/resource/en/reference_manual/rm0486-stm32n647657xx-armbased-32bit-mcus-stmicroelectronics.pdf#page=383). **USB decision, updated October 14:** USB supplies a PTP camera interface with explicit per-photo deletion, replacing drive emulation. Shutter presses do nothing. Download alone never deletes photos; D-40 selects 16-bit grayscale PNG with lossless compression on USB power. Keep the battery regulator disabled while USB powers the camera; source detection, reverse-current isolation and a hardware RUN inhibit still need implementation. Ignoring the button in firmware cannot suppress its direct divider-to-RUN path, and the HOLD diode does not establish reverse-power isolation. On the Nucleo, use the target MCU's CN8 USB port for PTP; CN10 is the ST-LINK port. **Busy-press decision, October 13:** ignore additional shutter presses while capturing or saving, finish the current photo, queue no delayed exposure and require a fresh press after ready. **Remaining implementation:** Apply the accepted failure map; complete downstream rails, regulator sizing/passives, shutdown timing and USB/battery source integration. **Recommendation:** Evaluate 1.8 V digital power within the selected architecture and qualify parts against the battery/load envelope. Qualify short taps, held/repeated presses, USB insertion/removal and shutdown during saving against the accepted policies. Boot failure/hang has no additional prototype recovery response; a stuck HOLD can keep the supply enabled. The proposed RUN/HOLD circuit needs the button held until firmware takes over and cannot turn off against a held button; 1.8 V changes its hold margin and RTC backup relationship. These need an integrated solution, not a regulator substitution. Close with a reviewed power-state sequence and voltage-domain schematic, followed by measured cold start, brownout, source transitions and controlled shutdown; boot/hang remains an accepted limitation. ([RUN/HOLD behavior](<../power.md>), [integration requirements](<../power.md>), [1.8 V consequences](<../docs/power/1v8-memory-assessment.md>))
