# STM32 camera startup error reports

As of **October 17, 2026**, other developers have reported STM32N6 CSI errors in our **`0x8000` D-PHY control-error class**. In one exact-code report, an ST employee connects startup synchronization errors with the camera starting after CSI initialization and describes them as nonblocking; that is useful context, not a guarantee covering every ECTRL event. ([ST discussion](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-embedded-software-32/dcmipp-silient-failure-158741)) Separately, ST has acknowledged the duplicate PHY-register write we found, but our isolated correction did not remove the warning. ([HAL issue #7](https://github.com/STMicroelectronics/stm32n6xx-hal-driver/issues/7)) **No matching silicon erratum, formal benign-startup exception, or demonstrated cure for our exact board/module was found** in the official documents inspected. The evidence supports investigating a startup transition while continuing to validate image integrity.

## Reports match the error class, not a proven cause

Our configuration is NUCLEO-N657X0-Q with CAM-6G3-073CLR, VD56G3 revision `0x3110`. Both our original ST-based smoke application and optimized firmware recorded `0x8000`; the newer instrumentation identifies physical data lane 1 before exposure. Five selected cold starts produced recognizable, complete first images with no observed payload CRC/ECC errors. This establishes usable capture on one unit, not electrical correctness or production reliability. ([Local investigation](../firmware/capture-timing/csi-startup-errors.md), [measured results](../firmware/capture-timing/optimization-results-2026-10-17.md))

The XS9950 result comes from indexed original posts/replies; direct page retrieval failed. Its later speculation that N6 requires non-continuous clock is contradicted by another author's reported successful continuous-clock IMX708 capture. Our own non-continuous-clock test also left the startup flag present. Treat clock mode as a device-specific diagnostic, not a blanket workaround. ([Continuous-clock report](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-products-25/stm32n6-dcmipp-csi-2-d-phy-does-it-support-non-continuous-gated-clock-clock-lane-never-locks-actclf-0-with-a-streaming-sensor-165957))

## Driver defects are real, but none explains our event

**HAL issue #7 remains open.** It identifies both oscillator-target bytes being written to E3 instead of low byte E2/high byte E3; ST acknowledged the report in September 2026. The issue provides no ECTRL reproduction or causal demonstration. Our isolated E2/E3 correction did not eliminate the flag, so it is not an established cure for our setup. ([Issue and acknowledgment](https://github.com/STMicroelectronics/stm32n6xx-hal-driver/issues/7))

Current HAL v1.4.0 also retains the wrong interrupt-disable macro in D-PHY handlers and hardcoded CCFR. Updating upstream alone does not supply a repair. Our corrected handler intentionally masks a source after its first callback, so callback silence cannot establish that the event stopped recurring. ST's MP25 Linux driver instead maintains error counters; that is useful diagnostic precedent, not N6 qualification. ([Pinned HAL handler](https://github.com/STMicroelectronics/stm32n6xx-hal-driver/blob/d88071ed0adc1991a5daed6f20ab311f80bb33b7/Src/stm32n6xx_hal_dcmipp.c#L3246-L3266), [Linux handler](https://github.com/STMicroelectronics/linux/blob/6d1d1c4c6647a0160114fbd4dbc91699b42ea687/drivers/media/platform/st/stm32/stm32-csi.c#L1119-L1147))

Two public clarification threads caution against speculative register changes: ST says NPWRDOWN=1 releases power-down, and confirms HAL's DLD=1 setting despite contradictory manual wording. Neither is an established fix for our flag. ([Power-down clarification](https://github.com/STMicroelectronics/stm32n6xx-hal-driver/issues/2), [DLD clarification](https://community.st.com/stm32-mcus-products-25/csi-pfcr-dld-bit-description-contradicts-hal-implementation-167440))

## Manuals constrain the next experiment without resolving it

**ES0620 Rev 5, August 2026, contains no CSI/D-PHY limitation entry.** AN6211 Rev 1 explains normal CSI transfers and integrity checks, while AN5967 Rev 6 supplies hardware guidance; neither documents this startup exception or a recovery procedure. Absence from those documents is a bounded search finding, not proof that ST lacks internal knowledge. ([ES0620](https://www.st.com/resource/en/errata_sheet/es0620-stm32n6xxxx-device-errata-stmicroelectronics.pdf), [AN6211](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/an6211-introduction-to-digital-camera-interface-pixel-pipeline-for-stm32-mcus-stmicroelectronics.pdf), [AN5967](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/an5967-getting-started-with-hardware-development-for-stm32n6-mcus-stmicroelectronics.pdf))

RM0486 defines ECTRL as an incorrect lane-state sequence. Its lane-merger configuration restriction requires CSIEN=0 and D-PHY_RX not streaming/providing a clock during mapping/lane-count changes. **This is not a blanket prohibition on sensor START preceding receiver enable:** compliance depends on actual clock activity during those writes. The sensor manual describes command states and clock modes, but does not expose the precise startup wire transition responsible for our event. ([RM0486 §40.6.2, §40.9.15](https://www.st.com/resource/en/reference_manual/rm0486-stm32n647657xx-armbased-32bit-mcus-stmicroelectronics.pdf), [UM2602](https://www.st.com/resource/en/user_manual/um2602-how-to-integrate-and-configure-the-vd56g3-vd66gy-and-vd16gz-devices-from-a-hardware-and-software-perspective-stmicroelectronics.pdf))

The next useful investigation is to preserve raw SR0/SR1 and interrupt enables across startup, rearm errors only within a bounded diagnostic window, and check receiver-clock state at merger configuration. Then vary receiver-enable timing while requiring the first frame and shortest required exposure to succeed. This would distinguish repeated faults from one startup transition without hiding either. **No such experiment, hardware change, or external outreach was performed during this literature review.** Search notes and retrieval limitations are retained in [the research folder](../research_notes/STM32%20camera%20startup%20error%20reports/).
