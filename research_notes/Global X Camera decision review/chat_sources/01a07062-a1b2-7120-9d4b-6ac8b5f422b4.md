## 2026-09-05T18:40:56.131Z | source line 1276 | assistant | 

**Cold-start and HOLD work while the button keeps rail− near BAT−. Direct GPIO shutdown is the problem.**

- Button closed: rail− = BAT− = 0 V. GPIO HIGH gives the NMOS approximately 3.3 V VGS, so it stays on after release.
- GPIO LOW means **0 V relative to rail−**, not BAT−. As rail− rises during attempted shutdown, the gate rises with it. For example, rail− at +2 V makes GPIO LOW—and therefore VGS—approximately +2 V. The NMOS conducts again. The circuit can settle with an elevated MCU ground and substantial current instead of shutting off.
- A gate pull-down to **rail−** makes this worse: it connects gate to drain through a resistor and tends to turn the NMOS on whenever rail− rises.
- A gate pull-down to **BAT−** establishes the correct OFF reference, but cannot override an actively driven GPIO LOW. With the GPIO high-impedance or unpowered, its ordinary lower protection clamp can also conduct from rail− into the gate, recreating the problem.

The gate needs a control interface referenced to **BAT−**; directly attaching a GPIO whose ground is the switched rail does not provide reliable OFF control.

“Autonomous” means the final power-down ordering must work after the CPU stops executing—it does **not** require a special sequencer IC. ST requires the auxiliary supplies, including the core supply, to fall below **0.6 V before the main supplies fall below their operating limits**. Three implementation choices substantially change the result:

- **Actual FMC timing:** if the original already operates at45 ns, the packed-image transfer difference falls to **0.685 ms**, assuming both full spare areas are written. FMC clock quantization, pulse widths and software/DMA overhead can prevent either device reaching its datasheet cycle minimum.
- **Spare bytes:** a128-byte physical spare area does not require transferring all128 bytes. Both remain BCH4-class devices; the firmware may transfer only its chosen parity/metadata positions while preserving bad-block markers.
- **Overlap:** cache programming may hide data loading behind prior-page programming, if implemented and allowed by device sequencing. CPU packing/ECC can also overlap NAND busy periods. None is implemented here, so do not credit this benefit yet.

A full-image readback adds another comparison term. At the fastest bus cycles and specified array-read maxima, packed-image readback is approximately **16.003→30.445 ms**, a **14.442 ms increase**. Verifying only status and commit metadata has much less read traffic; “verified commit” currently does not specify the algorithm.

Erasing four fresh blocks adds **4→14 ms typical**, or **14→40 ms using erase maxima**. Pre-erased blocks avoid this immediate save delay, but erase energy remains an amortized cost unless performed during USB-powered maintenance.

For energy, use input-referred power:

```text
Eboard = ∫ Pother_components(t) dt
         + ∫ [VNAND × INAND(t) / NAND_supply_efficiency(t)] dt
```

The MCU must retain the frame and control completion, but **need not remain fully awake during every NAND busy interval**. Sleep/interrupt-driven waits, DMA, clock reduction and promptly stopping the camera can materially reduce `Pother_components`. Those policies are currently unspecified.
