**A normal shutter click is far too small to power this camera. A deliberate squeeze, slide or winding action could plausibly provide enough energy, but would require a substantially different mechanism.**

For this envelope calculation, use **50 mJ—0.05 joules—per captured and saved photo**. That is an illustrative budget, not a measured requirement:

| Operation | Assumption | Energy |
|---|---|---:|
| Boot, camera setup and capture | 350 mW for 50 ms | 17.5 mJ |
| Processor during save and verification | 200 mW for 100 ms | 20 mJ |
| Flash, startup charging and margin | Allowance | 12.5 mJ |
| **Total** | | **50 mJ** |

Firmware optimization could reduce this; slower startup or storage could increase it. The estimates build on our [current budget](D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/power/current-budget.md) and [NAND calculations](D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/power/quad-nand-comparison.md).

The mechanical calculation is simple:

**Energy = average force × travel**, and **1 newton × 1 millimetre = 1 millijoule**.

Assuming an **optimistic 50% overall conversion into usable camera energy**:

| Motion | Mechanical work | Usable electrical energy |
|---|---:|---:|
| Light button: 3 N through 1 mm | 3 mJ | **1.5 mJ** |
| Firm button: 10 N through 5 mm | 50 mJ | **25 mJ** |
| Deliberate squeeze: 20 N through 10 mm | 200 mJ | **100 mJ** |

These are **average forces throughout the stroke**. A spring that starts unloaded and reaches the stated force only at the end stores half as much.

Our actual Omron shutter switch has roughly **1.59 N operating force and 0.2 mm pretravel**. Multiplying those gives only **0.32 mJ of mechanical work scale**, already about **160× short even with perfect conversion**. The switch itself does not generate electricity. [Omron datasheet](https://components.omron.com/eu-en/sites/components.omron.com.eu/files/datasheet_pdf/A162-E1.pdf).

Real miniature generators reinforce that conclusion: EnOcean’s ECO 260 delivers **0.12–0.21 mJ per push or release**. Collecting both would still require approximately **120–210 complete clicks** for our 50 mJ target, before further losses. [Manufacturer datasheet](https://www.enocean.com/wp-content/uploads/downloads-produkte/en/products/enocean_modules/ECO-260/ECO_260_Datasheet_Datasheet_Nov2025.pdf).

To get one photo from one motion at that optimistic 50% efficiency, we would need approximately:

- **1 mm travel:** 100 N average force—about 10 kg-force.
- **5 mm travel:** 20 N—about 2 kg-force.
- **10 mm travel:** 10 N—about 1 kg-force.

The last option is mechanically plausible for a slider or lever. Holding a stationary button longer adds no energy, and gearing only exchanges force for travel.

**My recommendation:** retain battery power for the present thin camera and light shutter action. If mechanical power is an appealing product feature, consider a **slide-to-charge or wind-to-charge action followed by a light shutter press**. That separates the forceful motion from exposure and allows a capacitor to supply power through the entire save. The first qualification step would be measuring actual joules per completed photo.
