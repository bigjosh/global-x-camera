**The Alliance part improves supply and cost, but it is not yet a proven battery-energy improvement.** Its lower voltage reduces NAND power, while slower transfers can keep the MCU powered longer.

Compared with our original **MX30LF2G18AC-TI**, one packed RAW10 image occupies **486,220 bytes / 238 pages**:

| Operation | Original 3.3 V NAND | Alliance 1.8 V NAND |
|---|---:|---:|
| Transfer image and full spare areas | 10.1 ms | 23.3 ms |
| Internal programming, typical | 71.4 ms | 71.4 ms |
| **Combined write estimate** | **81.5 ms** | **94.7 ms** |
| Combined using maximum program times | 152.9 ms | 189.9 ms |

These calculations assume pre-erased storage, sequential programming, and each chip’s fastest permitted bus timing. They exclude firmware/ECC overhead and verification. Sources: [Macronix datasheet](https://www.macronix.com/Lists/Datasheet/Attachments/8461/MX30LF2G18AC%2C%203V%2C%202Gb%2C%20v1.4.pdf), [Alliance datasheet](https://www.alliancememory.com/wp-content/uploads/AllianceMemory_AS9Fxx_Series_1Gb_2Gb_4Gb_8Gb_x8bits_SLC_Parallel_NAND_Fl.pdf).

**That apparent 16% slowdown depends heavily on our controller configuration.** If both chips operate at a practical 50 ns bus cycle, the estimates become **96.5 versus 97.3 ms**—almost identical.

For power, the original NAND specifies **15 mA typical while programming**, or **49.5 mW at 3.3 V**. Alliance specifies **20 mA maximum**, or **36 mW at 1.8 V**, but does not publish typical programming current. At the typical programming duration, those figures yield **3.53 mJ versus 2.57 mJ**. This mixes typical and maximum current, so it is an illustrative comparison—not a measured saving.

The whole-board effect can reverse that benefit. If the MCU and other consumers draw **200 mW**, an additional **13.25 ms costs 2.65 mJ**. That exceeds the approximately **0.96 mJ programming-energy reduction** in the example above. NAND transfer energy and the broader savings from moving everything to 1.8 V still need accounting.

Two other costs matter:

- Full-image readback adds approximately **16 ms versus 30 ms**.
- Erasing four blocks adds **4 ms versus 14 ms typical**. Keeping erased blocks ready—or erasing on USB power—avoids this during a battery capture.

**My recommendation remains to qualify Alliance**, while prioritizing packed RAW10, prompt camera shutdown, and efficient MCU waits. Actual FMC speed and firmware behavior will determine whether battery energy improves.

**Feasible, with a moderate pin/power redesign. I recommend evaluating 4-bit SDMMC2 at 3.3 V / 50 MHz first.** This preserves the 1.8 V CPU and NOR architecture, but adds a switched card supply and moves four simple existing signals. No files changed.

STM32N657 supports SDMMC1 and SDMMC2, SD/UHS-I, IDMA, and 4-bit SD transfers. Its silicon timing ceiling is 200 MHz at 1.8 V; actual interface speed must obey card mode, board loading and tuning. UHS-II is unsupported. For initial evaluation, 4-bit High Speed at 50 MHz has a **25 MB/s raw bus ceiling**, so our **486,220-byte RAW10 image needs at least 19.45 ms of bus data time**, excluding framing, card busy, initialization and filesystem work. At 25 MHz it is 38.90 ms. These are calculated interface lower bounds, not storage-write predictions. [DS14791 sections 3.18 and 5.3.21](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/stm32n657x0.pdf#page=202)

**Power implications**

- Ordinary microSD needs **2.7–3.6 V card VDD**, conventionally 3.3 V. **UHS 1.8 V refers to signals; card VDD remains at its higher supply.** AN5967 explicitly shows card power fixed at 3.3 V even in fast mode.
- For simple 3.3 V operation, give **VDDIO5 its own 3.3 V supply** and directly connect SDMMC2. The main MCU VDD and NOR can remain 1.8 V.
- UHS needs either a **3.3→1.8 V programmable I/O-bank supply** or a suitable SD translator. Card starts with 3.3 V signaling; firmware negotiates CMD11, switches voltage, configures bank range and tunes timing.
- The existing camera 2.8 V rail is not automatically a substitute: its regulator/current margin and 2.7 V minimum at the card during bursts would require separate qualification.
- Use a switched card supply with off-state isolation and adequate pulse current. Card pins/pull-ups must not back-power it after shutdown. [AN5967 pp.28–29 and 39–40](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/an5967-getting-started-with-hardware-development-for-stm32n6-mcus-stmicroelectronics.pdf#page=28)

**Bounded pin check against recovered connectivity**

| SDMMC2 signal | GPIO / ball | Current use |
|---|---|---|
| CK | PC2 / D9 | Accelerometer software-I²C SCL |
| CMD | PC3 / E10 | Accelerometer software-I²C SDA |
| D0 | PC4 / A10 | Accelerometer interrupt |
| D1 | PC5 / B10 | Unconnected |
| D2 | PC0 / C10 | Status LED |
| D3 | PE4 / D10 | Unconnected |

All six belong to **VDDIO5**. Move the accelerometer’s software I²C, interrupt and LED to spare 1.8 V GPIOs, including pins freed by deleting NAND. This is preferable to SDMMC1: its six main wires currently carry camera shutdown, MCLK, I²C4, analog-rail enable and RTC interrupt. Full pin assignment/routing remains to do. [DS14791 pin table and AF table pp.95–96,112,126,128](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/stm32n657x0.pdf#page=126)

**Cycle-time and firmware consequences**

SD cold initialization permits **up to one second from first ACMD41**; this is a protocol upper allowance, not a typical measured startup time. Specified write-busy intervals may reach **250 ms**, with certain SDXC final/stop busy periods up to **500 ms**. Speed-class labels do not bound one small image’s complete power-on/write/power-off latency. [SD Association physical specification §§4.2.3 and 4.6.2.2](https://www.sdcard.org/cms/wp-content/themes/sdcard-org/dl.php?f=Part1_Physical_Layer_Simplified_Specification_Ver6.00.pdf)

Because acquisition is buffered in RAM, **SD initialization can overlap camera initialization/capture** without placing SD startup on the 50 ms acquisition deadline. Start SD initialization asynchronously, prioritize the camera, shut the sensor off after capture, then complete storage. This overlap increases simultaneous peak load; staggering it is an available tradeoff if coin-cell droop dominates.

Firmware should use multi-block DMA writes to a preallocated contiguous file or reserved raw area, with aligned/cache-managed buffers. Finish filesystem synchronization and any supported card-cache flush, check final card status/ready, then remove power. A DMA completion callback is not by itself permission to switch power off. RM0486 provides busy-release signaling and interrupts, allowing CPU idle during waits; it also defines the shutdown/power-cycle sequence that prevents signal back-powering. [RM0486 §§30.6.6–30.8, pp.1422–1427](https://www.st.com/resource/en/reference_manual/rm0486-stm32n647657xx-armbased-32bit-mcus-stmicroelectronics.pdf#page=1422)

**Recommendation for measuring “best”:** compare complete cold-start-to-safe-off energy and latency, including aged/nearly-full cards and voltage/temperature corners. Benchmark both 50 MHz High Speed and UHS: for a half-megabyte image, initialization, metadata and internal programming may dominate, making higher signaling speed a poor return on hardware complexity.
