|---|---:|---:|

| Internal programming, typical | 71.4 ms | 71.4 ms |
| **Combined write estimate** | **81.5 ms** | **94.7 ms** |
| Combined using maximum program times | 152.9 ms | 189.9 ms |

These calculations assume pre-erased storage, sequential programming, and each chip’s fastest permitted bus timing. They exclude firmware/ECC overhead and verification. Sources: [Macronix datasheet](https://www.macronix.com/Lists/Datasheet/Attachments/8461/MX30LF2G18AC%2C%203V%2C%202Gb%2C%20v1.4.pdf), [Alliance datasheet](https://www.alliancememory.com/wp-content/uploads/AllianceMemory_AS9Fxx_Series_1Gb_2Gb_4Gb_8Gb_x8bits_SLC_Parallel_NAND_Fl.pdf).

**That apparent 16% slowdown depends heavily on our controller configuration.** If both chips operate at a practical 50 ns bus cycle, the estimates become **96.5 versus 97.3 ms**—almost identical.

For power, the original NAND specifies **15 mA typical while programming**, or **49.5 mW at 3.3 V**. Alliance specifies **20 mA maximum**, or **36 mW at 1.8 V**, but does not publish typical programming current. At the typical programming duration, those figures yield **3.53 mJ versus 2.57 mJ**. This mixes typical and maximum current, so it is an illustrative comparison—not a measured saving.

The whole-board effect can reverse that benefit. If the MCU and other consumers draw **200 mW**, an additional **13.25 ms costs 2.65 mJ**. That exceeds the approximately **0.96 mJ programming-energy reduction** in the example above. NAND transfer energy and the broader savings from moving everything to 1.8 V still need accounting.

Two other costs matter:

- Full-image readback adds approximately **16 ms versus 30 ms**.
- Erasing four blocks adds **4 ms versus 14 ms typical**. Keeping erased blocks ready—or erasing on USB power—avoids this during a battery capture.

**My recommendation remains to qualify Alliance**, while prioritizing packed RAW10, prompt camera shutdown, and efficient MCU waits. Actual FMC speed and firmware behavior will determine whether battery energy improves.

microSD is feasible, but **cold-start and safe-power-off latency can dominate a 486 kB photo**. Card MB/s ratings alone do not predict this camera’s energy or cycle time.

**Timing evidence from primary sources**

- SD initialization may take **up to 1 second after the first ACMD41**. This is an allowed maximum, not a typical time or a requirement to wait one second unconditionally. Further identification, bus configuration and filesystem work follow. [SD Association specification, §4.2.3](https://www.sdcard.org/cms/wp-content/themes/sdcard-org/dl.php?f=Part1_Physical_Layer_Simplified_Specification_Ver5.10.pdf)
- SDHC write busy can reach **250 ms**; SDXC/SDUC final write busy can reach **500 ms**. These are individual operation limits, not a whole-image completion guarantee. Avoid describing `file size ÷ rated speed` as a guaranteed save duration. [SD Association specification, §4.6.2](https://www.sdcard.org/cms/wp-content/themes/sdcard-org/dl.php?f=Part1_Physical_Layer_Simplified_Specification_Ver6.00.pdf)
- When the optional SD cache is enabled, busy can end before data reaches nonvolatile storage. The host must flush or use supported power-off notification; **cache flushing may take up to one second**. For the initial prototype, leave optional cache and card-initiated maintenance disabled unless the driver deliberately supports their shutdown contracts. [SD Association explanation](https://www.sdcard.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Mobile_Device_Innovations_20170227.pdf)

Our packed RAW10 frame is **486,220 B**, padded to **950 sectors / 486,400 B**:

| Effective payload rate | Payload time only |
|---:|---:|
| 10 MB/s | 48.64 ms |
| 20 MB/s | 24.32 ms |
| 25 MB/s | 19.46 ms |
| 50 MB/s | 9.73 ms |
| 90 MB/s | 5.40 ms |

At high-speed SD’s nominal 25 MB/s bus limit, protocol and busy overhead prevent attaining the exact 19.46 ms floor. A faster card may still reduce internal busy even where bus-limited.

Use:
```text
Cold-save time = initialization + metadata access
              + payload transfer + inter-block busy
              + metadata/commit + final ready/flush
```
If SD initialization starts alongside camera boot/acquisition, cycle time can hide their overlap:
```text
Cycle ≈ max(camera boot/acquisition, SD preparation) + save/commit + shutdown
```
SD initialization energy remains; overlapping does avoid charging the MCU’s already-required operating interval twice. Peak battery loading also increases during overlap.

**Numerical energy sensitivity — explicit assumptions, not card predictions**

Assume average SD current **100 mA at 3.3 V**, plus **200 mW** for the rest of the board during an additional storage interval: total **530 mW**.

| Illustrative initialization | Payload at 25 MB/s | Other busy/commit | Interval | Energy at regulated loads |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 ms | 19.46 ms | 10 ms | 49.46 ms | **26.2 mJ** |
| 100 ms | 19.46 ms | 50 ms | 169.46 ms | **89.8 mJ** |
| 1,000 ms | 19.46 ms | 250 ms | 1,269.46 ms | **672.8 mJ** |

These are sensitivity cases—not “typical”, “worst case”, or a complete camera cycle. Add filesystem/software overhead; adjust overlapping MCU time and measured per-phase current.

Every extra **100 ms costs 53 mJ** under this model. Raising transfer speed from 25 to 90 MB/s saves only **14.05 ms / 7.45 mJ** at unchanged power. Startup and commit therefore merit at least as much attention as transfer speed.

Alliance NAND’s existing partial baseline is approximately **21.5 mJ**: `200 mW × 94.7 ms + 2.57 mJ` programming. It excludes NAND transfer energy, verification and erase and mixes max current with typical programming duration. To match that partial reference, an isolated SD interval would need to finish within roughly **59/41/25 ms** at assumed SD average currents **50/100/200 mA** respectively. These are screening thresholds, not a proven NAND advantage.

At **85% converter efficiency**, the 530 mW example requires approximately **52 mA from a 12 V stack**, or **78 mA at 8 V**. Each of the four series CR2032s carries that current. That pulse demand needs verification separately from nominal battery energy.

**Firmware recommendation**

Use native four-bit SDMMC, DMA, aligned large multi-sector writes and packed RAW10. Preallocate contiguous storage while USB-powered; FatFs explicitly documents the benefits of contiguous allocation and warns against splitting multi-sector requests into single-sector writes. [FatFs allocation](https://elm-chan.org/fsw/ff/doc/expand.html), [FatFs application note](https://elm-chan.org/fsw/ff/doc/appnote.html)

For normal removable-card usability, use FAT with preallocation, then check successful `f_close()` and disk synchronization/actual card-ready before shutdown. `f_close()` already performs the file synchronization—calling `f_sync()` immediately before it is redundant. [FatFs synchronization](https://elm-chan.org/fsw/ff/doc/sync.html)

A fixed append-only raw log with CRC and recoverable commit records can reduce filesystem traffic, but needs host export software and **does not bypass the card’s internal flash management or make arbitrary power cuts safe**.

Qualify shortlisted cards by measuring **battery mJ from rail-enable to safe-off**, startup, first-image latency, final busy, and peak current over many cold cycles, including near-full/rewritten cards and temperature extremes. This workload determines the best card; continuous sequential benchmarks do not.
