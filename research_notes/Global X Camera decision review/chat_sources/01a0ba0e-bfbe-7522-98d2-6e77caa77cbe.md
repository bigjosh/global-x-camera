**Yes—quad SPI NAND deserves serious consideration. Some options are faster and better stocked than our parallel x8 candidate, but replenishment remains uncertain.**

For capacity, I recommend **4 Gbit / 512 MiB**. That accommodates roughly **800 full-resolution, packed RAW10 photos**, allowing 512 KiB per photo and reserving 20% for metadata, bad blocks and spare capacity. A 2 Gbit part would provide roughly 400 under the same assumptions.

The strongest candidates I found are:

| [Macronix MX35UF4G24AD-Z4I8](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/macronix/MX35UF4G24AD-Z4I8/24773874) — quad | 704 DigiKey / $20.33 | **49–53 ms at 100 MHz** |
| [Alliance AS9F14G08SA-45BIN](https://www.mouser.co.za/en/ProductDetail/Alliance-Memory/AS9F14G08SA-45BIN?qs=2SLPxufLcgCzKPiphQPyug%3D%3D) — parallel x8 | 837 Mouser / $8.40 | **95 ms** |

All are 4 Gbit, nominally 1.8 V. Stock checked September 8.

**The supply catch:** Mouser says Micron’s factory is currently **not accepting orders**, despite the substantial inventory. Winbond and Macronix have purchase limits—126 and 480 units per 30 days respectively—and no backorders. Quad offers more choices, but does not solve long-term supply by itself.

*These calculations use typical page-program times and pre-erased storage; they exclude verification, firmware overhead and metadata commits. Micron’s public datasheet also needs a current-revision check before selection.*

Quad is not inherently slower than x8. At 50 MHz it transfers 25 MB/s, slightly exceeding the Alliance part’s ideal 22.2 MB/s. Micron additionally programs **4 KiB pages**, needing half as many page operations. Micron and Winbond provide internal error correction; **Macronix requires host ECC**, adding firmware work and processing energy. [Micron datasheet](https://www.farnell.com/datasheets/3151594.pdf), [Winbond datasheet](https://media.digikey.com/pdf/Data%20Sheets/Winbond%20PDFs/W25N04KW_Ver_C_8-12-22.pdf), [Macronix datasheet](https://www.macronix.com/Lists/Datasheet/Attachments/9033/MX35UF4G24AD%2C%201.8V%2C%204Gb%2C%20v1.4.pdf).

**Power improves mainly through earlier shutdown.** Micron’s calculated internal programming energy is about **1.03 mJ/photo**, versus Winbond’s **2.68 mJ**. Alliance’s typical program current is unspecified, preventing a clean typical-energy comparison. If the rest of the camera consumes 200 mW while saving, Micron’s shorter write interval saves another **about 9 mJ** versus Alliance—an illustrative estimate, not measured battery savings.

**Full readback changes the comparison:** Micron reads more slowly. Including an entire verification read gives approximately **95–104 ms for Micron** versus **91–96 ms for Winbond**, before software overhead. Thus Micron’s write-only advantage largely disappears against Winbond when every photo is read back.

My recommendation is to **qualify Micron first for immediate availability**, retain Winbond for comparison, and keep Alliance as the much cheaper parallel fallback. Quad also simplifies routing to six interface signals.

## 2026-09-19T14:26:31.121Z | source line 73 | assistant | 

Two commercial benchmarks support **sub-millijoule electrical output per ordinary button action**, far below likely camera capture energy:

| Harvester | Mechanical actuation | Electrical output |
|---|---|---|
| **EnOcean ECO 260**, current product | 1.6–2.7 N; 1.5–1.6 mm travel; module 29.3 × 19.5 × 7 mm, 8 g | **120–210 µJ at 2 V per push or release**, with suitable power management at recommended 0–40 °C |
| **ZF AFIG-0007**, monostable | 5–12 N operating force; typical total travel 5.3 mm | **0.33 mJ per actuation**, measured into 100 Ω at 0.1 m/s and room temperature; regulated stored energy will be lower |

Sources:
- [ECO 260 datasheet, November 2025](https://www.enocean.com/wp-content/uploads/downloads-produkte/en/products/enocean_modules/ECO-260/ECO_260_Datasheet_Datasheet_Nov2025.pdf).
- [ZF specification](https://switches-sensors.zf.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2023_3-TS-AFIG-01_04.pdf), printed pp. 10–11 mechanical data, p. 17 energy test conditions, p. 19 pulse output.
- [ZF application note](https://switches-sensors.zf.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/220307-EnergyHarvester_US_Vers2-1.pdf), p. 4: actuation speed affects energy, faster operation reduces life.

Calculation cautions and implications:

- ECO 260 produces **0.24–0.42 mJ for push plus release**, assuming both pulses collected. The second pulse is unavailable until release. A **30 mJ** camera cycle would require roughly **72–125 complete clicks**, before further storage/regulation losses.
- ZF’s number is raw energy into a resistor, unlike ECO’s stated output with suitable power management. Do not compare them as equal usable DC energy specifications.
- Do **not** infer actual conversion efficiency from operating force × travel: operating force is a peak/tear-off value; mechanical input is \(\int F(x)\,dx\). ECO installation drawing confirms “tear-off force,” not constant force.
- For a hypothetical custom mechanism, **10 N average × 10 mm = 100 mJ mechanical**; an explicitly assumed 30% end-to-end conversion gives **30 mJ usable**. This is physically credible in an envelope sense, but requires a substantial long-travel squeeze/lever and a developed generator, not an ordinary shutter button.
- The camera should start only after sufficient stored energy is available. Springs/gearing reshape force and travel; they cannot increase total work. Holding a stationary button adds no mechanical energy.
- ECO 200 is marked not recommended for new designs; use ECO 260 as the benchmark. The old ECO 200 diagram’s ~82% must **not** be quoted as finger-to-DC efficiency.
