### Cell-level comparison

For these examples, the CR2032 stores **40% more nominal energy in 30% less physical volume**, giving approximately **2× the energy density**.

Those energy figures are estimates using nominal voltage, not measurements integrated over the discharge curve. For comparison, Energizer explicitly publishes **0.653 Wh/cm³** for its CR2032—close to the calculation above. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

### The important catch: discharge current

**The CR2032 capacity rating is measured at a very light load.** Panasonic specifies a standard drain of **0.2 mA**; Energizer’s capacity test uses approximately **0.19 mA**. That is a test condition, not an absolute maximum current, but it matters when interpreting the energy rating. Energizer’s discharge plots also show increasing internal resistance and greater voltage sag under pulses as the cell empties. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

In contrast, the example LiPo specifies **65 mA recommended maximum discharge and 130 mA maximum**—far more current-delivery capability. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

For your **130 mAh / 48-hour recorder target**, using the full nominal LiPo energy would correspond to an average battery-side power budget of:

\[
0.481\text{ Wh}/48\text{ h}\approx10\text{ mW}
\]

Delivering that power from a CR2032 would require roughly **3.3 mA at 3 V**, before accounting for differences in regulator efficiency—about **17× its capacity-test current**. Consequently, I would **not assume the full nominal CR2032 energy is usable in the recorder**, particularly with flash-write current bursts and a minimum operating-voltage cutoff. That needs validation under the actual load waveform. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="5"}

### Packaging also narrows the advantage

If the circular CR2032 requires a dedicated **20 × 20 × 3.2 mm rectangular space**, its effective volume becomes **1.28 cm³**. Its nominal energy density on that basis is **0.53 Wh/cm³**, so the advantage falls from **2× to about 1.6×**, even before adding a holder.

**Bottom line: about 2× by battery-body volume, or 1.6× by rectangular envelope for these examples. The usable-energy advantage at your recorder’s load could be substantially smaller.**
