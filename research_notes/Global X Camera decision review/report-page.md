## Current position

The recovered product concept is a thin, credit-card-size monochrome global-shutter camera: a shutter press starts the electronics, one full-resolution image receives time and orientation metadata, storage completes, and main power turns off. USB provides downloads independently of the batteries. The documented priorities are acquisition latency, total shot energy, thickness, area and procurability, in that order. These are the coherent existing baseline; this audit does not treat every sentence in the specification as a separately approved user decision. ([Product and priorities](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/spec.md:8>))

The Flux recovery and KiCad migration are real accomplishments. The reconstruction accounts for 148 components, 737 terminals and 81 nets; it preserves the recovered connectivity and records its additions. A readable edition also exists. **On October 12, a fresh export of the current schematic matched the September reviewed connectivity baseline.** The current parts remain STM32N657X0H3Q, W25Q128JVPIQ boot NOR, MX30LF2G18AC-TI parallel NAND, TPS62132/TPS62825 regulators and AO3401A battery switches. The readable edition rearranges presentation; it does not implement the proposed fixes. ([Reconstruction](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/hardware/reconstruction/README.md:3>), [readable edition](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/hardware/kicad-readable/README.md:7>), [fresh evidence](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/research_notes/Global X Camera decision review/current-cad-evidence.json>))

The September register contains **47 findings: 5 blockers, 22 high, 17 medium and 3 low**. It distinguishes confirmed conflicts, conditional requirements and matters requiring measurement; these are not 47 independent product decisions. No completed routed PCB, manufacturing release, integrated camera firmware or measured complete capture/save/shutdown cycle was found. The zero-error ERC result demonstrates consistency of the reconstructed schematic model, not a working or manufacturable camera. ([Findings register](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/review/findings.md:3>), [review limits](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/DESIGN-REVIEW.md:47>))

## Open questions

This is a decision inventory, **not a request to answer every question at once**. Recommendations below are this review’s proposed course, not recovered approvals. The first three establish what the camera must do; storage and power then need a joint decision.

### 1 Camera choice for the first prototype

**Question:** Retain VD55G0, or require a different resolution/module before proceeding? **Recommendation:** Retain the existing VD55G0 baseline for the first feasibility prototype unless a specific unmet image requirement justifies changing it. The STM32N6 decision remains settled either way. The complication is that adopting VD56G3 or VD5943 affects memory fit, storage capacity, boot/capture timing, energy, optics and mechanics. Close this item with an explicit resolution/image-quality requirement and named module/lens, then regenerate dependent budgets. ([Existing module](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/spec.md:33>), [recent comparison](https://chatgpt.com/c/6acd261d-d2f0-83ea-bac0-a86e92a61bc2))

### 2 Fast capture acceptance criteria

**Question:** What event ends the 50 ms target, and under what light/exposure conditions? **Recommendation:** State separately shutter-to-exposure, shutter-to-complete-frame and complete save/shutdown time; nominate one as the acquisition acceptance limit. Preserve saving outside the 50 ms deadline. Cold boot, sensor revision/patch, exposure and first-frame behavior all matter: the archived review finds that one sensor revision’s documented patch path alone exceeds 50 ms. Close this with identified module samples, a small capture-first boot path and measured timestamps under the chosen light conditions. ([Cold-start review](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/DESIGN-REVIEW.md:29>), [camera revision evidence](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/review/camera-memory-review.md:68>))

### 3 Battery thickness and OFF current requirements

**Question:** Are four CR2032s and ≤5 mm mandatory, and is <1 µA a guaranteed maximum or a measured target over a specified environment? **Recommendation:** Keep them as goals while defining temperature, cell end-of-life, shelf life, minimum useful battery state and acceptable retry behavior. Require representative measurements before freezing topology. A 3.2 mm cell plus 1.6 mm PCB already consumes 4.8 mm before contacts and covers; series cells all carry the same shot current. The LTC3621 proposal’s regulator maximum alone reaches the nominal whole-board limit at its cited test point. Close this with a sectioned mechanical model, cell/contact pulse tests and a complete leakage budget including RTC backup separately. ([Battery and mechanics review](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/DESIGN-REVIEW.md:27>), [OFF budget](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/power.md:142>))

### 4 Storage and data integrity

**Question:** Which storage device/interface, exact minimum image count and durable-save policy should the next board implement? **Recommendation:** Qualify the proposed 1.8 V quad-NAND path, retaining a qualified alternative, before declaring a winner. Use the final image format and a common complete-save test, including metadata, ECC/status checks, verification and shutdown. Decide full readback, power-interruption recovery, unpowered retention, response to corrected errors, behavior when full and refresh opportunities during USB sessions. The tradeoff is important: Micron’s modeled write-only advantage nearly disappears against Winbond when full readback is included, and longer MCU-on time can outweigh lower NAND energy. microSD adds a switched 3.3 V rail and socket, with cold-start and durable-commit behavior needing qualification. Close with exact-part documentation/supply and representative fault-injection and energy results. ([Quad NAND comparison](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/power/quad-nand-comparison.md:73>), [whole-system energy](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/power/nand-energy-impact.md:50>), [reliability concerns](https://chatgpt.com/c/6aa5d95a-b2e8-83ea-933a-5f18d637cad3), [microSD assessment](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/power/microsd-feasibility.md:3>))

### 5 Power and shutter control

**Question:** Choose the rail topology, regulator capacity, startup/hold control and USB/battery behavior together. **Recommendation:** Evaluate 1.8 V digital power as the leading redesign candidate, then select parts against the agreed battery/load envelope. Define short taps, held/repeated presses, firmware crashes, USB insertion/removal and shutdown during saving. The proposed RUN/HOLD circuit needs the button held until firmware takes over and cannot turn off against a held button; 1.8 V changes its hold margin and RTC backup relationship. These need an integrated solution, not a regulator substitution. Close with a reviewed power-state sequence and voltage-domain schematic, followed by measured cold start, brownout, source transitions and bounded shutdown. ([RUN/HOLD behavior](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/power.md:60>), [integration requirements](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/power.md:222>), [1.8 V consequences](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/power/1v8-memory-assessment.md:55>))

### 6 Manufacturing and prototype package

**Question:** Which assembler, real stackup, layer count, prototype quantity and mechanical assembly? **Recommendation:** Use JLCPCB Standard as the first quoting/escape-study candidate, with PCBWay as fallback; attempt conventional four layers and compare six if necessary to preserve sound routing. Confirm current exact-part availability, camera procurement, panel/rail requirements and assembly scope before commitment. The September report’s stock observations are dated, and the card outline needs accommodation for its reported Standard-service panel minimum. Close with a current quote/BOM, a successful BGA escape trial and camera/battery section model. ([Manufacturing study](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/manufacturing/china-pcba-feasibility.md:3>), [fabrication fit](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/manufacturing/china-pcba-feasibility.md:56>))

### Optional product and firmware branches

**Flash:** Is it needed in the first camera? The flash discussion’s 2–3 AA cells and rapid high-brightness requirements did not replace the base camera’s coin cells. **Recommendation:** Defer integration unless first-prototype image requirements demand it; close scope with brightness, distance, volume and latency criteria before selecting a power source. **Directional light sensing:** Is the requested narrow photopic field of view part of camera exposure control? **Recommendation:** Establish that role before adding hardware; optics, angular definition, thickness, calibration and startup remain unresolved. ([Flash conversation](https://chatgpt.com/c/6a887bc5-aa48-83ea-bda2-31a0fa35fd91), [light-sensor conversation](https://chatgpt.com/c/6ab35647-1ad4-83ea-b316-ded648b2583b))

**Firmware language/framework:** Rust support was explored, not selected. **Recommendation:** Recover the existing firmware design first, then prove the required boot/camera/storage path in the chosen stack; do not let a language preference hold up justified schematic corrections. **Shutter harvesting:** The model rejects an ordinary click as sufficient and proposes deliberate wind/squeeze/slide mechanisms only for further investigation. **Recommendation:** Keep that as a separate product experiment until measured shot energy supports reopening it. ([Rust conversation](https://chatgpt.com/c/6aa98729-e8c0-83ea-ba82-8e311d32e7c4), [harvesting assessment](<D:/Github/global-x-camera/docs/power/shutter-energy-harvesting.md:3>))

## Recommended discussion order

The concise todo sequence is: **sensor/first-prototype scope → capture acceptance limits → battery, thickness and OFF-current envelope → joint storage and power choice → manufacturing package → optional features and firmware-stack decisions**. Recover existing firmware and prepare justified corrections in parallel; missing Claude history is not a gate.

Start only with whether the recent sensor comparison should change the first prototype. **My recommendation is to retain VD55G0 unless an explicit unmet image requirement warrants the additional design work.** Once that is settled, record the decision and move to timing. This gives the subsequent energy and storage studies a stable workload without reopening the agreed STM32N6 direction.
